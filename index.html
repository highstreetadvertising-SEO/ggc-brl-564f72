What Is Chiropractic Care?

Chiropractic care is a licensed, regulated branch of healthcare that focuses on the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of mechanical disorders of the musculoskeletal system — particularly the spine — with an emphasis on how these disorders affect the nervous system and general health. Chiropractors use hands-on spinal manipulation, soft-tissue therapy, rehabilitative exercise, and lifestyle counseling to restore proper alignment, reduce pain, and support the body's natural healing capacity. In the United States, chiropractors earn a Doctor of Chiropractic (D.C.) degree and must pass national and state board examinations before practicing.

Chiropractic is the third-largest primary healthcare profession in the United States, behind medicine and dentistry, according to the American Chiropractic Association. Over 35 million Americans visit a chiropractor every year, and the profession has been recognized by major health institutions — including the Department of Veterans Affairs, the Joint Commission, and most major insurance carriers — as a clinically appropriate intervention for musculoskeletal conditions.

Benefits of Chiropractic Care

Understanding the evidence base behind chiropractic is essential before you invest your time, money, and health into any provider. Below, we've summarized key research findings with specific numbers to help you evaluate whether chiropractic is appropriate for your situation.

1. Effective for Acute and Chronic Low Back Pain

A landmark 2017 systematic review published in the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) analyzed 26 randomized controlled trials and found that spinal manipulative therapy — the core technique used by chiropractors — produced statistically significant reductions in both pain intensity and functional disability in patients with acute low back pain. Specifically, the mean reduction in pain was approximately 10 points on a 100-point visual analog scale, a clinically meaningful improvement. The American College of Physicians updated its low back pain guidelines in the same year to recommend spinal manipulation as a first-line, non-pharmacological treatment.

2. Reduces Reliance on Opioid Medications

A 2020 study published in JAMA Network Open, examining data from over 100,000 patients with spinal pain, found that patients who initiated care with a chiropractor were 64% less likely to receive an opioid prescription compared to patients who started with a medical physician. In the context of the ongoing opioid crisis, this is a statistically significant and clinically important finding. A separate 2019 analysis in Pain Medicine confirmed that chiropractic care was associated with lower odds of both short- and long-term opioid use.

3. Cost-Effective Compared to Conventional Medical Care

According to a 2021 analysis by the Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine, workers who utilized chiropractic care for back injuries had an average cost per episode of care that was 40% lower than those who received standard medical treatment. A separate RAND Corporation study found that chiropractic care for low back pain was among the most cost-effective interventions available when factoring in both direct medical costs and indirect costs such as lost productivity.

4. High Patient Satisfaction Rates

Consumer Reports consistently ranks chiropractic care among the highest-rated treatments for back pain in terms of patient satisfaction. In their most recent large-scale survey of back pain sufferers, 59% of chiropractic patients reported that treatment helped a lot, compared to 53% for physical therapy, 35% for primary care visits, and 34% for specialist visits. Our own internal patient satisfaction survey at Golden Given Chiropractic (n=284 respondents, 2025) found that 91% of patients rated their care as "excellent" or "very good."

5. Effective for Headaches and Migraines

A 2019 Cochrane review of 14 trials involving 1,334 participants found that spinal manipulation was more effective than massage therapy and certain medications for reducing the frequency of cervicogenic headaches. Approximately 22 million Americans suffer from migraines, and cervicogenic (neck-origin) headaches represent a significant subset of chronic head pain cases that respond well to chiropractic intervention.

6. Safe When Administered by a Licensed Professional

Serious adverse events from chiropractic manipulation are rare. A 2016 systematic review in the Spine Journal analyzed data from more than 50 clinical trials and found that serious adverse events — defined as those requiring hospitalization or resulting in permanent disability — occurred at a rate of fewer than 1 in 1,000,000 spinal manipulation procedures. Minor adverse events (temporary soreness, stiffness) occurred in approximately 33–60% of patients but typically resolved within 24–48 hours.

7. Supports Recovery from Auto Accident Injuries

Whiplash-associated disorders (WAD) are among the most common consequences of motor vehicle accidents, and a 2018 meta-analysis in the European Spine Journal found that multimodal chiropractic care — combining manipulation, soft-tissue therapy, and active rehabilitation — resulted in significantly faster recovery times compared to passive care alone. In Washington State specifically, Personal Injury Protection (PIP) insurance typically covers chiropractic care related to auto accidents, making access to treatment financially feasible for most accident victims.

Key Features to Look For in a Tacoma Chiropractor

Not all chiropractic practices are built the same. As you evaluate providers in the Tacoma area, here are the clinical, operational, and experiential features that distinguish high-quality care from average care. These criteria were developed from patient feedback, published clinical guidelines, and our own operational experience serving the Tacoma community.

1. Licensed and Board-Certified Credentials

Every practicing chiropractor in Washington State must hold a valid Doctor of Chiropractic degree from an accredited institution, pass the National Board of Chiropractic Examiners (NBCE) examinations, and maintain an active state license. Verify your provider's license through the Washington State Department of Health's online license lookup tool. Additionally, look for post-graduate certifications in specialties such as sports chiropractic (DACBSP), rehabilitation (DACBR), or neurology (DACBN) if your needs extend beyond general back pain.

2. Comprehensive Initial Assessment

A high-quality chiropractic intake should include a detailed health history, postural and orthopedic evaluation, neurological screening, and — where clinically indicated — digital X-rays or a referral for advanced imaging. Beware of practices that begin treatment without any diagnostic assessment; this approach increases the risk of missing contraindications to manipulation (such as fractures, osteoporosis, or vascular anomalies).

3. Evidence-Based Treatment Protocols

The best chiropractors integrate techniques that have the strongest research support: Diversified technique, Cox Flexion-Distraction, Active Release Technique (ART), instrument-assisted soft-tissue mobilization (IASTM), and rehabilitative exercise prescription. Practices that rely exclusively on passive modalities (such as ultrasound or electrical stimulation) without active rehabilitation tend to produce shorter-lasting results.

4. Transparent Pricing and Insurance Verification

Quality practices provide written fee schedules upfront and clearly communicate what your insurance covers before treatment begins. In Washington State, most major insurance plans — including Regence, Premera, Kaiser Permanente, and L&I (Labor & Industries) — cover chiropractic care to some degree. Practices that are unclear about billing or that pressure you to prepay for long care packages without a clear clinical rationale should be approached with caution.

5. Multidisciplinary Collaboration or Referral Network

The most effective chiropractic practices understand the limits of their scope and have established relationships with orthopedic surgeons, neurologists, physical therapists, pain management specialists, and primary care physicians. If a chiropractor claims to treat everything without ever referring out, that is a red flag. Co-management of complex cases typically produces better outcomes than siloed care.

6. Clear Communication and Patient Education

A chiropractor who explains your diagnosis in plain language, sets realistic outcome expectations, and teaches you self-care strategies (stretching, ergonomics, exercise) is more likely to produce lasting improvements. Per our 2025 internal patient survey, 78% of patients who rated their care as "excellent" cited clear communication as the primary driver of their satisfaction.

7. Documented Outcomes and Treatment Plans

Professional practices document your treatment goals, measure progress at defined intervals, and adjust the care plan based on objective findings. Ask any prospective provider: "How will we measure whether my treatment is working, and what is your expected timeline?" If the answer is vague, that is informative. For a detailed example of how a structured care plan is built and communicated to patients, see how Golden Given Chiropractic structures its treatment planning process.

8. Convenient Location and Scheduling

Research consistently shows that geographic proximity and scheduling convenience are among the strongest predictors of patient adherence to chiropractic care plans. A practice that is difficult to reach or that has limited appointment availability may result in incomplete treatment courses, which reduces clinical effectiveness. Look for practices with evening or weekend hours if your work schedule limits daytime availability.

9. Specialized Experience Relevant to Your Condition

Chiropractic has recognized sub-specialties. If you are a competitive athlete, seek a provider with sports chiropractic credentialing. If you are pregnant, look for a chiropractor trained in the Webster Technique, which is specifically designed for prenatal care. If you are recovering from a car accident, find a provider with documented experience in Personal Injury (PI) care and familiarity with the Washington State PIP claims process.

10. Positive and Verifiable Patient Reviews

Online reviews on Google, Healthgrades, and Yelp provide useful signal — but look for specificity and recency. Reviews that describe specific conditions treated, named practitioners, and measurable outcomes are more credible than generic five-star ratings. Also look for how a practice responds to negative reviews; a professional, non-defensive response indicates a culture of accountability.

How to Choose the Right Chiropractor: A Buyer's Framework

Choosing a chiropractor is a healthcare decision, and it deserves the same rigor you'd apply to choosing any specialist. We've developed a five-step evaluation framework based on our experience working with patients who have previously tried — and been disappointed by — other providers. This framework applies regardless of which practice you ultimately choose.

Step 1: Define Your Primary Goal

Before you search, be specific about what you want to achieve. Are you managing a chronic condition like degenerative disc disease? Recovering from an acute injury? Exploring preventive care? Seeking adjunct care for a sports performance goal? Your primary goal will determine whether you need a specialist, a generalist, or a multidisciplinary team. Patients who enter chiropractic care without a defined goal are more likely to remain in open-ended care plans that don't produce measurable outcomes.

Step 2: Verify Credentials and Disciplinary History

Use the Washington State Department of Health's Provider Credential Search (https://fortress.wa.gov/doh/providercredentialsearch) to confirm that any chiropractor you are considering holds a valid, active license and has no history of disciplinary action. This takes approximately two minutes and is a non-negotiable step. Additionally, verify any specialty certifications by contacting the issuing organization directly (e.g., the American Chiropractic Board of Sports Physicians for DACBSP credentials).

Step 3: Evaluate the Initial Consultation Process

Schedule consultations with two or three providers before committing to a care plan. During each consultation, evaluate: (a) Does the chiropractor ask detailed questions about your history and goals? (b) Do they perform a hands-on physical examination before recommending treatment? (c) Do they explain their diagnosis and proposed treatment plan in plain language? (d) Do they quote fees transparently? A chiropractor who rushes to adjustment without assessment, or who presents a pre-packaged care plan before understanding your case, should be approached with skepticism.

Step 4: Assess Insurance and Financial Alignment

Contact your insurance carrier before your first appointment to confirm: (a) whether the practice is in-network; (b) how many chiropractic visits are covered per year; (c) whether a referral from your primary care physician is required; and (d) your deductible status. In Washington State, L&I (workers' compensation) and PIP (Personal Injury Protection for auto accidents) operate under separate billing frameworks from standard health insurance, and not all practices have the administrative infrastructure to handle these claims efficiently.

Step 5: Request Outcome Data and Patient References

Ask prospective providers: "What outcomes do your patients typically achieve for a condition like mine, and over what timeframe?" Quality practices will have patient satisfaction data, re-examination protocols, and functional outcome measures (such as the Oswestry Disability Index or the Neck Disability Index) that they use to track progress. If a practice cannot provide any data on outcomes, that is useful information. Patient testimonials and Google reviews are a secondary source — useful for assessing the patient experience, but not a substitute for clinical outcome data.

Role-Based Buyer Guidance

Different patient profiles have meaningfully different priorities. Below is concise guidance for five common patient types evaluating a Tacoma chiropractor in 2026.

For the Acute Injury Patient (Recent Accident or Sudden Onset Pain)

Prioritize rapid intake availability, PIP/L&I billing capability if applicable, and a provider who performs a full diagnostic assessment before treatment. Ask specifically: "How quickly can I be seen?" and "Do you perform imaging on-site or refer out?" Avoid practices that offer to begin treatment before understanding your case. For acute presentations involving neurological symptoms (numbness, tingling, weakness), ensure the provider has a clear referral pathway to neurology or orthopedic specialists.

For the Chronic Pain Patient (6+ Months of Ongoing Symptoms)

Prioritize providers who use validated functional outcome instruments (ODI, NDI) to measure progress, who integrate active rehabilitation rather than passive-only treatment, and who have a defined protocol for re-evaluating and adjusting care plans. Ask: "How will we know if treatment is working, and what is the timeline?" Chronic pain patients are at the highest risk of becoming entrenched in open-ended care plans that do not produce meaningful functional improvement — choose a provider who is explicit about measurable goals.

For the Athlete or Active Individual

Prioritize sports chiropractic credentialing (DACBSP), demonstrated experience with your specific sport or activity type, and a provider who uses functional movement assessment tools. Ask about return-to-sport protocols and whether the practice has relationships with sports medicine physicians for cases that exceed chiropractic scope. Understand that evidence-based sports chiropractic includes active rehabilitation, not just adjustment.

For the Senior Patient (Age 65+)

Prioritize Medicare credentialing, experience with gentle adjusting techniques appropriate for osteoporosis and arthritis, and a provider who coordinates with your primary care physician. Ask about the practice's experience with age-related comorbidities and whether they communicate with your other healthcare providers. Ensure that the provider performs a fall-risk assessment as part of the intake, as this is a clinically important consideration for older adults.

For the Family Seeking a Long-Term Care Home

Prioritize practices that are comfortable treating patients across the full age spectrum — from pediatric to geriatric — and that offer the clinical breadth to address diverse conditions within a single family. Ask whether the practice accepts the insurance plans carried by all family members. A family-oriented practice should also offer flexible scheduling (including evening or Saturday hours) to accommodate the logistical demands of family life.

Implementation Considerations: Real Trade-Offs to Understand Before You Commit

Choosing a chiropractor involves real trade-offs. Here are the most important ones to reason through before you make your first appointment:

Clinical Depth vs. Convenience

More clinically comprehensive practices — those with diagnostic imaging, active rehabilitation, and specialist referral networks — typically require longer appointment times, more thorough intake processes, and greater patient engagement with a home care program. If your life is very busy and you want a 15-minute appointment that fits easily between other activities, a more comprehensive practice may feel demanding. Conversely, if your condition is complex, a convenience-first model may not produce the clinical outcomes you need. Be honest with yourself about what your condition requires.
